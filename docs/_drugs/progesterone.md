# Progesterone: From Progesterone Deficiency to Uterine Corpus Cancer

## One-Sentence Summary

Progesterone is a natural steroid hormone conventionally used for progesterone-deficiency–related gynecological conditions (e.g., luteal-phase support, secondary amenorrhea, hormone replacement therapy). The TxGNN model predicts potential efficacy in **Uterine Corpus Cancer**, but the current evidence base is preliminary — drawn from **35 clinical trials** and **17 publications**, most of which address related menopausal/hormone-replacement contexts or histopathology rather than direct progesterone treatment trials for this cancer.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, progesterone activates the progesterone receptor (PR), inducing endometrial stromal decidualization and glandular differentiation while antagonizing estrogen-driven proliferation. This receptor-mediated mechanism underlies the established use of progestins across several hormone-responsive gynecological conditions.

Uterine corpus cancer and endometrial cancer share the same anatomical site and hormonal biology — most uterine corpus malignancies are estrogen-driven, and progestins (e.g., megestrol acetate, medroxyprogesterone acetate) are an established therapeutic class for endometrial hyperplasia and early-stage, low-grade endometrioid endometrial cancer, particularly in fertility-sparing treatment. This provides a plausible mechanistic rationale for the TxGNN prediction.

However, the specific evidence set gathered under the "uterine corpus cancer" label is weaker than what exists under the closely related "endometrial cancer" prediction (a separate, higher-ranked candidate in this evidence pack, scored at evidence level L2 with a "Proceed with Guardrails" recommendation). Most trials retrieved here concern perimenopausal vasomotor symptoms, hormone replacement therapy, or menopausal endometrial protection rather than direct progesterone-based treatment of the cancer itself — consistent with the L3 evidence level and "Research Question" stage assigned to this specific prediction.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for progesterone specifically in **uterine corpus cancer** is preliminary (L3, decision stage S1, "Research Question") — most retrieved trials and literature address perimenopausal hormone therapy, endometrial protection, or unrelated histopathology rather than direct progesterone treatment of this cancer. A **Blocking** data gap (missing NPRA/package-insert warnings and contraindications) also prevents completion of the required S1 safety pre-assessment. Notably, the closely related "endometrial cancer" prediction in this evidence pack carries stronger support (L2, "Proceed with Guardrails"), suggesting the repurposing signal may be better substantiated under that framing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- NPRA package insert data (warnings, contraindications) to resolve the Blocking data gap (DG001)
- DrugBank/mechanism of action detail to resolve the High-severity data gap (DG002)
- Malaysia product registration details (license number, product name, approved indication text — currently blank in the evidence pack)
- Consideration of re-scoping this candidate toward the "endometrial cancer" prediction, which has a stronger direct evidence base
- Route-of-administration compatibility assessment for the target population
