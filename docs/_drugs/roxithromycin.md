## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for roxithromycin is not available. Based on known information, roxithromycin belongs to the macrolide antibiotic class, which acts by reversibly binding the bacterial 50S ribosomal subunit to inhibit protein synthesis. Its efficacy in common bacterial infections has been well established for decades.

Leprosy is caused by *Mycobacterium leprae*, a bacterium, so a protein-synthesis-inhibiting antibiotic is mechanistically plausible as an anti-leprosy agent. Several older in vitro and mouse-model studies found that macrolides — including roxithromycin — suppress *M. leprae* growth and reduce inflammatory cytokines, giving the prediction a biological rationale.

However, the same literature consistently shows that clarithromycin outperforms roxithromycin against *M. leprae*, and roxithromycin has never entered standard leprosy treatment guidelines (which rely on dapsone/rifampicin/clofazimine, with clarithromycin as a second-line option). No human clinical trials of roxithromycin in leprosy have been conducted. The mechanistic plausibility is therefore real but weak, and unsupported by any clinical validation.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for leprosy is limited to decades-old in vitro/animal studies and reviews, with no clinical trials in humans and no inclusion in leprosy treatment guidelines; a related macrolide (clarithromycin) is already documented as more effective, weakening the case for roxithromycin specifically. A blocking safety data gap (missing TFDA label warnings/contraindications) also prevents entry into the S1 safety pre-assessment stage.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/NPRA package insert warnings, precautions, and contraindications (currently a blocking data gap)
- Documented mechanism of action (MOA) data from DrugBank or equivalent source
- Head-to-head or dose-ranging data comparing roxithromycin vs. clarithromycin for anti-mycobacterial activity
- Any human clinical evidence (even case reports) before advancing beyond the research-question stage
