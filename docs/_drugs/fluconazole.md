## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action (MOA) data is not available for this candidate (DG002, data gap). Based on known pharmacology, fluconazole is a triazole antifungal that inhibits fungal cytochrome P450 14α-demethylase (ERG11), blocking ergosterol synthesis and disrupting fungal cell membrane integrity.

The original indication text could not be extracted from this evidence pack because all TFDA license entries are blank. However, esophageal candidiasis — the top predicted indication — sits within the same *Candida* infection spectrum that fluconazole's antifungal mechanism directly targets (mucosal candidiasis, including oropharyngeal, esophageal, and vulvovaginal forms, as well as invasive candidemia). This mechanistic fit is consistent with the strength of the clinical trial evidence below, where fluconazole already appears as an active comparator/standard-of-care arm in multiple registered trials for esophageal candidiasis rather than purely as a novel hypothesis.
