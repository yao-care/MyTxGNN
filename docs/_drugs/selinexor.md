## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for selinexor is not yet available in this evidence pack, and the original indication is also unrecorded. Based on the repurposing rationale attached to this prediction, selinexor is understood to be a selective inhibitor of XPO1 (exportin-1/CRM1). Mechanistically, XPO1 inhibition can block nuclear export of transcription factors such as NF-κB, and the NF-κB/RANKL pathway is involved in osteoclast differentiation — so an indirect effect on bone metabolism is biologically plausible.

However, this link is purely theoretical at present. There is no direct clinical or preclinical evidence connecting selinexor to drug-induced osteoporosis specifically. Selinexor is also known to cause systemic adverse effects (e.g., hyponatremia, nausea, weight loss), and the net direction of its effect on bone density (protective vs. harmful) is not established from available data — a worsening of bone loss cannot be excluded.

Because the original indication and MOA are unconfirmed, the relationship between the original and predicted indications cannot be meaningfully assessed at this time. This prediction should be treated as an early hypothesis generated by the model, not as evidence of therapeutic relevance.
