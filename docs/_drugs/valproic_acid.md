## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed DrugBank mechanism-of-action data for valproic acid was not available in this evidence pack (data gap DG002). However, the collected literature consistently describes valproic acid as a broad-spectrum AED that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels and increases brain GABA concentration, raising the seizure threshold across multiple seizure types (PMID 30592252).

Visual epilepsy — seizures triggered by photic or visual stimuli — is a **reflex subtype of epilepsy**, the same disease category in which valproic acid already has decades of established clinical use. Mechanistically, this is less a "new" repurposing target than an extension into a specific epilepsy phenotype: the drug's existing anticonvulsant action plausibly generalizes to photosensitive/reflex triggers, and several of the identified trials and case reports (e.g., hyperekplexia/startle response to VPA, PMID 2496695) support anticonvulsant efficacy in reflex-triggered seizure syndromes more broadly.

That said, none of the 4 identified clinical trials directly enroll or test valproic acid specifically for visual/photic epilepsy — they cover residual amblyopia (different indication), post-TBI epilepsy prevention, a different drug (ropinirole) in myoclonic epilepsy, and AED effects in pregnancy. The literature similarly consists largely of general epilepsy/VPA pharmacology reviews rather than photosensitive-epilepsy-specific studies. The L2 evidence rating should therefore be read as "well-supported within the parent indication (epilepsy)" rather than "directly validated for this specific reflex subtype."

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- Valproic acid is already a marketed, well-characterized antiepileptic drug in Malaysia; extending its use to reflex/situational epilepsy subtypes such as visual epilepsy is mechanistically consistent with its existing approved indication rather than a novel therapeutic area, and several adjacent reflex-epilepsy case reports support anticonvulsant benefit.
- However, no identified trial or publication directly and specifically validates valproic acid for visual/photic epilepsy — evidence is inferred from the parent epilepsy indication, and 8 of the other 9 screened candidate indications lack sufficient support (Hold or early Research Question stage).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/NPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap DG001) before any safety sign-off
- DrugBank-sourced mechanism of action data (DG002) to strengthen the mechanistic rationale
- NPRA license numbers, product names, and approved indication text (currently blank in this pack) to confirm current labeled scope
- A prospective study or registry analysis specifically targeting photosensitive/reflex epilepsy outcomes with valproic acid, rather than relying on general-epilepsy literature
