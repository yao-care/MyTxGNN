# Eptinezumab: From Migraine Prevention to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Eptinezumab is an anti-CGRP (calcitonin gene-related peptide) monoclonal antibody used generally for migraine prevention. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective specifically for **migraine with brainstem aura**, but this direction is currently supported only by **post-hoc/subgroup literature and general CGRP-pathway reviews (8 publications)**, with **no clinical trials registered** for this specific subtype.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed structured mechanism-of-action data for eptinezumab is not available in this evidence pack. Based on the available evidence, eptinezumab is a humanized anti-CGRP monoclonal antibody, and CGRP is a core mediator in migraine pathophysiology, including in migraine with aura.

Unlike triptan-class drugs, anti-CGRP monoclonal antibodies do not have vasoconstrictive activity. Theoretically, this makes them more suitable for the brainstem aura subtype (formerly called basilar-type migraine), a population in which vasoconstrictive agents are typically avoided or contraindicated. This mechanistic rationale is the basis of the TxGNN prediction.

However, the supporting literature — including the PROMISE-1/PROMISE-2 post-hoc analysis — was conducted in patients with migraine and self-reported aura generally, not specifically in patients meeting ICHD-3 diagnostic criteria for brainstem aura. The remaining evidence consists of general CGRP-pathway reviews and case reports. The mechanistic extrapolation to this specific subtype is therefore reasonable but not yet directly demonstrated.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for the brainstem aura subtype specifically is indirect — a post-hoc subgroup analysis (self-reported aura, not ICHD-3-confirmed brainstem aura) plus general CGRP-pathway reviews and a case report, with no dedicated clinical trials. Additionally, the required TFDA/NPRA label warnings and contraindications data is missing, which blocks the S1 safety screening step.
