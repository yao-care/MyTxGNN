## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for vasopressin is not available in this dataset. Based on general pharmacology, vasopressin (AVP) acts primarily on V1/V2 receptors and is used clinically for diabetes insipidus, GI variceal bleeding, and vasodilatory (septic) shock.

The evidence pack's own repurposing rationale raises a significant concern rather than confirming plausibility: the supporting literature actually concerns **desmopressin (DDAVP)**, a synthetic vasopressin analogue that acts on V2 receptors to trigger endothelial release of von Willebrand Factor and Factor VIII — a mechanism used in Factor VIII/vWF deficiency states, not prothrombin (Factor II) deficiency. Prothrombin is not stored in Weibel-Palade bodies and is not released by DDAVP stimulation. The rationale explicitly notes this predicted link may reflect the knowledge graph conflating vasopressin with its analogue DDAVP, or proximity between coagulation-factor nodes, rather than a genuine pharmacological relationship.

Given this, the prediction should be treated as a hypothesis generated by graph proximity rather than a mechanistically grounded signal.
