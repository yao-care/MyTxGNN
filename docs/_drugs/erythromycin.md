## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available (flagged as a Data Gap). Based on known pharmacology, erythromycin is a macrolide antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding the 50S ribosomal subunit; topically and systemically it has also been shown to exert anti-inflammatory and anti-neutrophil-chemotactic effects independent of its antibacterial action.

Acne vulgaris has a well-documented inflammatory and *Cutibacterium (Propionibacterium) acnes*-driven component. Erythromycin's dual antibacterial/anti-inflammatory activity is mechanistically consistent with acne pathophysiology, and topical erythromycin has in fact been used clinically as an acne treatment for decades (e.g., akne-Mycin), though its use has declined due to rising antimicrobial resistance. This existing real-world use pattern lends plausibility to the TxGNN prediction, even though the propagated score for this candidate is reported as 0.00%, which limits how much weight can be placed on the model output alone.
