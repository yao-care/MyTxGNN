## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the evidence pack. Based on known pharmacology, rivastigmine is a cholinesterase inhibitor (inhibiting both acetylcholinesterase and butyrylcholinesterase), and its efficacy in Alzheimer's disease dementia has been established; mechanistically it may also be applicable to glaucoma through its cholinergic effects.

Rivastigmine belongs to the same pharmacological class as classic cholinergic glaucoma agents (e.g., pilocarpine, physostigmine). Cholinergic agents lower intraocular pressure (IOP) through ciliary muscle contraction and increased aqueous outflow via the trabecular meshwork — a mechanism historically used in glaucoma treatment. This provides a plausible pharmacological bridge between rivastigmine's known CNS cholinesterase-inhibiting action and a potential ocular hypotensive effect.

A direct animal study (PMID 10673128) demonstrated that topical rivastigmine lowered intraocular pressure in rabbits, providing preclinical support for extending this class effect to rivastigmine itself. However, the mechanistic link is currently supported only by preclinical and review-level literature, with no human clinical trial evidence for glaucoma.
