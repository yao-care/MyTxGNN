# Sirolimus: From Renal Transplant Rejection Prophylaxis to Liposarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Sirolimus (rapamycin) is an mTOR inhibitor originally used for prophylaxis of renal transplant rejection. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Liposarcoma**, with **5 clinical trials** and **12 publications** currently supporting this direction — though most direct trial evidence uses sirolimus analogs (temsirolimus, everolimus, ridaforolimus) rather than sirolimus itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for Sirolimus is not available (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on known information, Sirolimus belongs to the mTOR inhibitor class, an established drug family used for prophylaxis of organ transplant rejection by suppressing T-cell proliferation. Several sirolimus-family molecules — temsirolimus (an ester prodrug of sirolimus), everolimus, and ridaforolimus (all mTOR inhibitors) — are already approved or extensively studied as sarcoma therapies, providing strong class-level precedent.

Mechanistically, dedifferentiated liposarcoma has been shown to exhibit activation of the Akt-mTOR and MAPK signaling pathways (PMID 26518767), giving a biological rationale for mTOR blockade in this tumor type. Direct clinical support for sirolimus itself (not just analogs) comes from NCT02821507, a completed Phase 2 single-arm trial combining sirolimus with cyclophosphamide in metastatic/unresectable myxoid liposarcoma and chondrosarcoma (n=70) — the strongest sirolimus-specific evidence in this pack.

It is also worth noting that the same mTOR mechanism underlying this liposarcoma hypothesis is already clinically validated for other conditions evaluated in this evidence pack: lymphangioleiomyomatosis (FDA-approved use, L1 evidence), benign PEComa/angiomyolipoma (L2, with the sirolimus-family drug nab-sirolimus FDA-approved for malignant PEComa), and clear cell renal carcinoma (temsirolimus/everolimus approved, L2). This broader pattern strengthens confidence in the underlying mTOR-inhibition mechanism, even though liposarcoma-specific evidence for sirolimus remains at an earlier "Research Question" stage.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (Research Question — Decision Stage S2)**

**Rationale:**
Sirolimus-family mTOR inhibitors have plausible mechanistic and Phase 2 clinical support in liposarcoma, but direct evidence for the sirolimus molecule itself is limited to a single completed Phase 2 combination trial (NCT02821507); most other trials use analogs (temsirolimus, everolimus, ridaforolimus). This matches the evidence pack's own S2 "Research Question" staging rather than a guarded-proceed recommendation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for Sirolimus (currently a High-severity data gap)
- NPRA label warnings/contraindications (currently a Blocking data gap for safety review)
- Product-level Malaysia registration details (license number, brand name, dosage form, indication text)
- Additional sirolimus-specific (not just analog) trial data in liposarcoma to strengthen the evidence level beyond L2
