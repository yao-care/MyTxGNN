## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, guaifenesin is an expectorant that stimulates a gastric mucosal vagal reflex, which in turn increases respiratory tract gland secretion and reduces mucus viscosity. This is the classical basis for its use in productive cough.

The nasal cavity and the lower respiratory tract share a continuous mucosal secretory system, so a drug that thins and mobilizes mucus in the airway has a plausible, if indirect, mechanistic path to relieving conditions involving retained nasal secretions, such as chronic rhinitis or sinusitis. This is not a direct anti-inflammatory or anti-allergic mechanism, but a secretion/viscosity-based rationale.

This plausibility is partially borne out by a completed pediatric Phase 2 pilot trial (NCT01364467) testing oral guaifenesin specifically in chronic rhinosinusitis, and by older literature describing guaifenesin's adjunctive use in sinusitis and allergic upper-airway conditions. The evidence base is small and largely supportive/indirect rather than confirmatory.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for the nasal cavity disease prediction is preliminary — a single small (n=30) completed pediatric pilot trial plus two indirect, low-tier literature sources — and does not yet meet a bar for progression. Critically, TFDA/NPRA label warnings and contraindications are marked as a **Blocking** data gap (DG001), which prevents even an initial safety screening (S1), so no guarded proceed decision can be responsibly made yet.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings, contraindications, and DDI profile (DG001, blocking)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank (DG002)
- A larger, confirmatory trial beyond the existing n=30 pediatric pilot study
- Itemized Malaysia/NPRA license and indication text for the 23 registered products (currently blank in this data pull)
