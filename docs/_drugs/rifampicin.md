# Rifampicin: From Tuberculosis to Conjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Rifampicin is a rifamycin-class antibiotic historically used to treat tuberculosis and other mycobacterial infections (e.g., leprosy, meningococcal prophylaxis). The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Conjunctivitis**, with **no registered clinical trials** but **20 supporting publications** — largely historical case reports and an older controlled trachoma trial — currently available.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, rifampicin is a rifamycin-class antibiotic that inhibits bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, and its efficacy against Mycobacterium tuberculosis and other mycobacterial and gram-positive/gram-negative pathogens has been proven; mechanistically this broad-spectrum bactericidal activity may be applicable to bacterial conjunctivitis.

Conjunctivitis can be caused by a range of bacterial pathogens including *Chlamydia trachomatis* (trachoma), *Neisseria meningitidis*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, and *Corynebacterium* species — several of which are also susceptible to rifampicin's antibacterial mechanism. This is not a purely novel hypothesis: rifampicin (topical and systemic) has actually been used clinically and studied since the 1970s for trachoma and for meningococcal conjunctivitis, giving the TxGNN prediction some historical grounding despite the lack of modern trial data.

However, the supporting literature is overwhelmingly older (1970s–1990s), consists mostly of case reports/case series rather than confirmatory trials, and no clinical trials specifically testing rifampicin against conjunctivitis are currently registered. The prediction should therefore be treated as a plausible, mechanistically-supported hypothesis rather than a well-established repurposing candidate.
