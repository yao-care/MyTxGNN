## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available (data gap DG002). Based on known information, zonisamide is part of the antiepileptic drug (AED) class — a benzisoxazole-derivative sulfonamide that acts via Na⁺/T-type Ca²⁺ channel blockade and, to a lesser extent, dopaminergic/serotonergic modulation. Its efficacy in epilepsy has been well established through decades of clinical use.

For Tourette syndrome, the proposed rationale rests on zonisamide's dopamine-modulating activity, which is theoretically analogous to other AEDs occasionally used off-label for tic disorders. However, this is a purely theoretical mechanistic link — the evidence pack itself explicitly notes that no clinical trials or literature currently exist to support this specific indication. This prediction should therefore be treated as a hypothesis-generating signal rather than an evidence-backed repurposing candidate.

It is worth noting that this evidence pack also contains lower-ranked but far better-supported predictions for the same drug — notably **absence epilepsy** (Evidence Level L1, Phase 3 RCT with n=583, "Proceed with Guardrails") and **manic bipolar affective disorder** (Evidence Level L2, one RCT plus a systematic review, "Research Question"). These represent stronger near-term repurposing opportunities than Tourette syndrome and may warrant separate evaluation.

Other entries in this evidence pack (predicted indications for methemoglobinemia and related enzyme deficiency) flag that sulfonamide-class drugs are a **known risk factor for inducing methemoglobinemia**, rather than a treatment for it.
