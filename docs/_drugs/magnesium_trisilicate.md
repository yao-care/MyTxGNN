# Magnesium Trisilicate: From Antacid Therapy to Esophagitis

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this product (DrugBank MOA: Data Gap). Based on known pharmacology, Magnesium Trisilicate belongs to the antacid drug class — it reacts with gastric hydrochloric acid to raise intragastric pH and reduce pepsin activity. This acid-neutralizing action is the therapeutic basis of its established antacid use.

Esophagitis, particularly reflux-related esophagitis, shares the same acid-driven pathophysiology as classic antacid indications: mucosal injury results from repeated exposure to acidic gastric refluxate. Mechanistically, reducing gastric acidity and pepsin activity could plausibly lessen esophageal mucosal irritation, which is the rationale the evidence pack itself cites for this prediction.

However, the supporting evidence is thin and dated. The single registered trial (NCT03883074) tests a combination device (hyaluronic acid + chondroitin sulphate + magnesium trisilicate) rather than magnesium trisilicate alone, and most of the literature consists of 1980s–1990s comparative studies of alginate/antacid mixtures versus sucralfate — not modern, magnesium-trisilicate-specific RCTs with esophagitis as a primary endpoint.
