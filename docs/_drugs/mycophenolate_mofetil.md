# Mycophenolate Mofetil: From Transplant Rejection Prophylaxis to HIV Infectious Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) is an immunosuppressant established for prevention of organ transplant rejection. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **HIV infectious disease**, with **10 clinical trials** and **20 publications** identified, though most are small, older studies with inconsistent or unconfirmed outcomes.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism-of-action data for this drug is not available in the Evidence Pack (flagged as data gap DG002). Based on known pharmacology, MMF is a prodrug of mycophenolic acid, a selective, reversible inhibitor of inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH) that blocks de novo purine (guanosine) synthesis, preferentially in activated T and B lymphocytes. Its established efficacy is in suppressing lymphocyte proliferation to prevent transplant rejection.

Mechanistically, this same IMPDH-inhibiting, anti-proliferative effect reduces the pool of activated CD4+ T cells — the primary target cells for HIV replication — and depletes intracellular deoxyguanosine triphosphate (dGTP), which several studies proposed would potentiate nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (e.g., abacavir) and reduce viral reservoir size.

This hypothesis was tested in a series of small Phase 1/2/4 studies between 2001 and 2015 (adjunct to antiretroviral therapy, in ART-naive patients, and during treatment interruption), but results were inconsistent, sample sizes were small, and some trials were withdrawn or reached unknown completion status. The prediction is mechanistically plausible but has not translated into demonstrated clinical benefit or regulatory approval for HIV.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Mechanistic and preliminary clinical evidence for HIV as a repurposing indication is plausible but weak — most direct trials are small, several are withdrawn or of unknown status, and no RCT has established clinical benefit. More critically, the absence of NPRA/package-insert warnings and contraindications (DG001, Blocking) means the mandatory safety initial evaluation (S1) cannot yet be completed, which precludes a Go or Proceed-with-Guardrails recommendation regardless of efficacy signal strength.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- NPRA-approved package insert: warnings, contraindications, and DDI data for Malaysia-registered MMF products (resolves DG001)
- Confirmed mechanism of action from DrugBank (resolves DG002)
- Complete license-level market data (product names, dosage forms, approved indication text) for the 12 Malaysia registrations
- Assessment of whether more recent/larger HIV-MMF trials exist beyond those captured in this pull, given most identified studies are >15 years old
