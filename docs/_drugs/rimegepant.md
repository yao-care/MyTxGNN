# Rimegepant: From Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Rimegepant is a small-molecule CGRP receptor antagonist originally approved for the acute treatment of migraine (with or without aura) and the preventive treatment of episodic migraine. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**, a migraine-aura subtype, and this direction is currently supported by **14 relevant publications** but **no dedicated clinical trials**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Structured mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank is currently a data gap. Based on the supporting literature in this evidence pack, rimegepant is a small-molecule calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor antagonist — a second-generation "gepant" — approved for acute treatment of migraine with or without aura and for prevention of episodic migraine in adults.

The original and predicted indications sit within the same disease spectrum: migraine with brainstem aura (formerly termed basilar-type migraine) is a recognized aura subtype of migraine, sharing the CGRP-mediated neurovascular pathophysiology implicated in migraine generally. This is a mechanistically direct extension within the same disease family, not a cross-indication leap.

Because rimegepant acts via CGRP receptor blockade rather than vasoconstriction, it is theoretically not subject to the safety concerns (vascular constriction) that have historically excluded triptans from use in brainstem-aura migraine. That said, no trial has specifically enrolled or evaluated this aura subtype, so the mechanistic rationale remains extrapolated rather than directly demonstrated.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link between rimegepant (CGRP receptor antagonism) and migraine with brainstem aura is direct and biologically plausible, supported by 14 publications on rimegepant's efficacy/safety in migraine broadly, but no trial has specifically studied this aura subtype — hence guardrails rather than a full Go.

The other five TxGNN-predicted candidates (atrophoderma vermiculata, ulerythema ophryogenesis, heparin cofactor 2 deficiency, antithrombin deficiency type 2, factor 5 excess with spontaneous thrombosis) have no literature or trial support and no known mechanistic connection to CGRP biology — these are recommended **Hold** and are not pursued further in this report.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/NPRA label warnings and contraindications (currently blocking — DG001)
- Structured DrugBank mechanism-of-action data (DG002)
- A dedicated study or subgroup analysis in patients with migraine with brainstem aura, since existing evidence is extrapolated from migraine in general
